# Bloom — build log

Working notes on what was built and why. Rough on purpose; raw material for the case study.

---

## Foundation

- **One `index.html`, no build step.** The file gets opened straight from disk about as often as it gets served, so anything on a relative path 404s there. The basemap is inlined as a base64 data URI for the same reason. Costs ~270KB; worth it.
- **Map is a baked screenshot, not a live map.** CARTO dark tiles, stitched and cropped, reskinned with a CSS filter chain, pins projected onto it from lat/lng via Web Mercator. Never waits on tiles. `tools/` regenerates it.
  - CARTO Positron was too flat to reskin — land and roads are both near-white, so any cream treatment erases the streets. Dark Matter's luminance range is what makes the maroon work.
  - A `mix-blend-mode` overlay (the tidier technique) does **not** composite over a tile layer. Had to use a filter chain.
- **One caption treatment, and one gap around it.** The small grey explaining line existed three times — `.flow-sub` at 12.5px, `.sizes-sub` at 13.5, `.about-note` at 12.5 with a *negative* top margin to claw back a heading's gap — each with its own idea of the spacing. Two of them ended up flush against the thing they described: the tag count sat directly on the tag control, and the radius sentence directly on the map. Now 13px / 1.5 everywhere with 10px of air, from `--cap-size` / `--cap-lead` / `--cap-y`.
  - **The gap is on top only, and that's structural rather than tidy.** A flex row establishes a formatting context, so the empty chip row left standing while the size field is open blocks a *bottom* margin on a caption from collapsing into the field's. Give the caption one and the accessories field opens lower than the chip it replaced — precisely the bug the old `margin:0` was guarding against, and the same trap recorded further down. Whatever follows a caption owns the gap under it instead. Re-verified 0px on both axes across all seven types after the change.
  - Two inline `style=` margins went with it, including the one setting "Post will appear as:" 2px off its own value.
- **One text-entry surface, everywhere.** White, hairline `--line` border, 8px corners — the same box a chip wears, because the composer's size and tag fields *are* chips that grew. There were four treatments before: a Material underline on the composer's fields, the same underline with different padding on location, a cream pill for the profile's tag finder, and the About editor with no box at all. The app bar's search pill is the one deliberate holdout — it's chrome sitting on ink, not a field on a form.
  - The underline was a leftover from when those fields had floating labels and helper text under them; both were removed a while back, so the convention was carrying nothing.
  - The tag finder used to match the search pill on the reasoning that "finding a tag feels like the one place you already find tags". The composer's tag field does the same job and is the closer sibling, so it matches that instead.
- **Everything sits on a 4px spacing grid.** Audited and snapped 92 off-grid values in one pass. Deliberate exceptions: a 1px border overlap on the profile tabs, and the map callout's −29px tail offset (that's pin radius + arrow, not spacing).
- **Phone frame + status bar shell**, scaled to fit whatever window it's in.
- **A mock iOS keyboard, because there isn't one on a desktop.** Focus a field and it rises; press `K` to raise it on any screen, including ones with nothing to focus. It sits *outside* `.app` and shrinks it from the bottom rather than covering it — which is what iOS actually does, and means footers lift, scroll areas shorten and the nav bar rides up with no keyboard-specific rules anywhere in the app.
  - It found something the first time it went up: the composer's Next floats over its own body, which is fine at full height and lands on the field you're typing in once 291px is gone. It steps out while the keyboard is up. The sheets' footers are in flow and lifted on their own.
  - Keys swallow their own pointerdown so pressing one can't blur the field the keyboard belongs to.
  - **The field scrolls itself above the keyboard.** Tapping "+ add tags" near the bottom of the composer used to leave the field behind the keyboard, with no hint that scrolling would find it. Now the scroller moves it into view — measured after the app has finished shrinking, since mid-transition it's still full height and the field measures as perfectly visible. A field with a caption under it brings the caption too: the tag entry's hint is the line that says what to type. And a box taller than what's left above the keyboard shows its *top* rather than chasing its bottom, which would push the first line you're typing off the screen.
  - **A Done button, in an input accessory bar above the keyboard** — the app's own bar, so it wears our berry rather than the system blue. That's where iOS puts it, it's always under your thumb, and it doesn't compete with the page's own Save or Next. Which matters here: the composer's Next hides while the keyboard is up, so without this there was no visible way forward at all while typing.
  - Return had been doing double duty as the dismiss. Now it does what Return does — commits the tag or size you've typed — and Done owns putting the keyboard away. Done sets the keyboard down itself rather than relying on the blur to bounce back through `focusout`.

## Navigation

- **Tabs are roots, drill-ins push.** Reaching a tab clears the trail so there's no back arrow; drilling into a listing or someone's profile records what it covered and earns one.
- **Back retraces instead of always going home.** A listing opened from your profile returns to your profile; the same listing opened from the feed returns to the feed.
- **The lit nav tab doesn't move when you drill in.** Opening someone's profile from a feed listing used to light the Profile tab even though you were three levels into Home.
- **Scroll position is preserved on back.** Only the arriving screen resets. Zeroing every screen made the feed jump to the top *while it was still on screen sliding away*.
- **Push transition**: 300ms, `cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1)`. The outgoing screen drifts 25% left rather than sitting frozen — that's what sells depth. Reverses exactly on back.

## Persistent search bar

- Lifted the app bar **out of the screens** into app-level chrome so it holds still while pages slide under it.
- **The leading glyph sits beside the pill, not inside it.** Hamburger (view menu) at a root, back arrow once you've drilled in — the slot still changes meaning rather than the bar changing shape. On the bar rather than on the pill it takes the bar's colour, sky on ink.
- **One token pair holds the two bars together.** `--lead-w` and `--lead-gap` set the slot, and the search screen's drop card starts at `16px + lead + gap` rather than at a hardcoded 52. Home's pill and search's pill measure identically — same left, same right, same height — which is the constraint that lets the search screen open over home without the pill moving a pixel.
- **The full-width pill belongs to the menu, not to the screen.** With the menu down the glyph steps aside and the pill grows into its slot; with the menu shut this screen is just results, so the bar reverts to home's exactly — glyph back, pill at the same 52px. Dismissing the menu used to leave a full-width pill with nothing to its left and no way out, which read as a broken version of home.
- **Three trailing glyphs, never two exits at once.** Something typed → a ringed x that empties the field. Nothing typed, menu down → a bare x, because the arrow has stepped aside and this is the only way out. Menu shut → the magnifier, because the arrow is back and owns the exit.
- **The pill's width is not animated, and that was the whole jank.** Growing it from home's 52px to 16px meant the panel spent the open at the *narrow* width, where the category chips wrap into three rows instead of two — 41px of height change happening underneath a height animation. It also poisoned the measurement: the grid's offset is taken once, at open, from the card's height, so the results were parked 41px too low for as long as the menu stayed down. The card is at its final width before anything is measured or animated. Gap from card to first heading: 69px → 28px.
- **The card and the grid share a curve, not just a duration.** The grid rides the card's bottom edge, and the two used to disagree on the way out — the panel accelerating away on an ease-in while the grid eased out — so they arrived at different moments. Both directions now name one duration and one easing.
- **The drop panel's rows line up with the text in the pill, not with the card's edge** — 20px inside the card rather than 16, matching the pill's own inset. Measured: input and every heading, chip row and scrolling row all land on 36px.
- This let the listing's own header go entirely, including a `...` that had never done anything.
- **Layering rule: only the *front* screen may ride above the bar.** First attempt applied it to any bar-less screen, which put a *departing* profile on top of the listing arriving underneath it — it hung there covering the page and then blinked out. Front = arriving on a push, departing on a pop.
- **The bar travels with the page that owns it, but only when the other page has no bar.** Home ↔ listing: static (the whole point of persistence). Profile → listing: arrives *with* the listing, sharing its keyframes so they can't drift apart.

## Search

- **Tag-based, not free-text-first.** A dropdown card hangs over the results; typing swaps the card's contents in place rather than opening a different surface.
- **Results are always on screen.** An unfiltered search is just everything near you — which is what you were already looking at. Adding a tag narrows the same grid instead of replacing a blank page with one.
- **The corpus is everyone else's closet.** Vocabulary, suggestion counts, and results all read from one list, so a suggestion can never promise items the results won't show. You can't swap with yourself anyway.
- **No dead ends, by construction.** Swept every category and tag; where the data had gaps (dresses and outerwear lived only in my own closet) I added listings rather than letting a chip return nothing. Adding Hats later meant adding two hats for the same reason — a new category isn't done until someone owns one.
- **Type-ahead walks the data, not a synonym table.** Typing "lea" surfaces *Leather*, then *Workwear, Utility, Broken in* — tags that co-occur on the listings that matched. Relatedness comes from the listings, so it can't rot.
- **Free text in saved tags — reversed.** The rule was "you can only save a tag that exists", to avoid a saved tag that finds nothing. But a saved tag isn't a search result, it's a statement of what you want, and *shearling* is a legitimate thing to be looking for on a day nobody has posted one. Refusing it made the list a description of the current inventory rather than of you. The suggestions still lead with what the data answers — a tag with a count beside it finds something today — and what you typed sits above them.
- Typing a word the data already knows resolves to its real casing, so "vintage" doesn't sit next to *Vintage* as a second entry, and the typed chip is suppressed when the exact word is already on offer below. Chips and the Enter key run the same path.
- **The styles section is gone entirely.** "Trending styles" was a scrolling list of tags; replacing it with an add-tag field only made the duplication obvious, because the search bar *is* that field. One text input per card. The panel is down to categories and, once you pick one, its sizes.
- **Enter in the search bar resolves against the vocabulary.** A word the data knows lands as that tag — matched case-insensitively, so "vintage" arrives as *Vintage* with exact-match filtering and 7 results, exactly as tapping the chip used to. Anything else stays a quoted term. Same code path for Enter and for the typed chip in the type-ahead, so the two can't disagree.
- **Saved profile tags filter home** rather than opening a separate screen. Deleted a whole screen mode. The back arrow lives in the filter row — which only exists while a filter is on, which is exactly when there's somewhere to go back to. Costs no chrome.
- **Two animations at once on open**: the menu sweeps down (260ms) while the page behind crossfades (300ms) to the same listings, filtered and already clear of the menu. The dissolve is what carries the shift — a clip reveal wiped a *finished* layout into view, so the movement had already happened behind the wipe.

## Profile

- **One screen, two designs.** Yours: Listings/Faves/About, private sizes, an edit on every section. Theirs: Listings/About, Message as the primary action, no Faves, no sizes, no edits.
- **"My sizes" → "Size preferences", and the privacy note is pinned in the head** rather than sitting in the scroll. "Only you can see this" is a standing fact about the screen, not an intro you scroll past — and it was previously buried in a sentence explaining *why* we ask, which the screen doesn't need to justify.
- **More than one size per system.** Plenty of people sit between two and wear both; a filter that only ever knew one of them hid half of what would fit. Stored as arrays and sorted into tier order on save — "M, L" is a size range, "L, M" is a list of two things you happened to press. Old single-value saves are read back as one-item lists.
- **The cards stayed, and the wrap was fixed at the source.** I'd tried removing the cards to cure a stray sixth letter dropping to its own row; the cards weren't the cause. Wrapping flex was: it fills each row to the brim and strands whatever's left, so six letters came out 5+1 and ten waists 6+4. Each tier is now a grid whose column count is chosen so the rows divide as evenly as they can — 6 across for letters, 5+5 for waists, 5+4 for shoes — and equal columns stop "XS" from being as wide as "XXL". The cap on columns comes from the longest label in the tier, which is why the numeric tiers get more of them. Shoes run 5–13 in half steps — 17 chips, 6+6+5.
- **Shoes stayed chips rather than becoming a dropdown**, which was the obvious reach once half sizes made the list 17 long. A dropdown returns one value, and this screen is multi-select on purpose — shoes most of all, since being an 8 in one brand and an 8.5 in another is the normal case, not an edge one. `<select multiple>` isn't a real mobile control, and iOS's wheel picker is single-value by definition, so the dropdown would have quietly revoked something we'd just built. A slider is worse on every axis: no discrete labels, fine motor control, and it can only say a value or a contiguous range, which "8 and 9.5" isn't. Chips are also the most accessible of the three — each is a real labelled button with a large target. The price is 36px of scroll.
- **Cut sits at the top of the screen, above the size systems**, because it qualifies everything below it: an M in a men's tee is not an M in a women's one, so you read the cut before the letters rather than after. It's a list here and a single value on a post — a garment has one cut, a person can shop across two. No "any" chip: picking nothing already means you don't mind, and an explicit "any" would only make that a thing you have to remember to press.
- Bottoms keeps its rule between the letter and waist tiers — they're two ways of saying the same thing, and the line is what says so. With multi-select you can answer in both.
- **Every vertical scroller fades at an edge that still has content past it** — `.fade-scroll` with `.fade-t`/`.fade-b`, driven exactly like the chip strips' `.fade-l`/`.fade-r`. An edge that fades is the only thing telling you the list didn't just stop where the header cut it.
  - Started as a cream overlay on the sizes screen only, on the reasoning that a mask "would fade to whatever sits behind the screen". That was wrong: every one of these scrollers is transparent on its own screen's ground, so a mask fades to that ground — cream on the editors, white on the feed — without being told which. Generalising it removed a wrapper div and a hardcoded colour.
  - The overlay version also faded at rest, dimming the first row of a list you hadn't scrolled. Tracking scroll position fixes that, and it's the convention the strips already used.
  - **The top fade is opt-in, not universal** — `.fade-under`, meaning the chrome above the scroller is the same colour as the ground below it. That's only the editors: a cream head meeting a cream body with no rule between them, where the fade is genuinely the one thing saying content continues. Everywhere else something already marks that edge — the maroon app bar against white content, the profile's ruled tab bar — and fading there just hazed the first row of cards for no information. Applying it everywhere was the mistake in "all instances"; the rule was never about scrollers, it was about edges that aren't otherwise drawn.
  - **The bottom fade switches off while the keyboard is up.** That edge is no longer running under anything — it butts straight onto the keyboard's own chrome, which is already a hard boundary and one you can't drag past. Fading into it doesn't read as "more below", it just smudges the line sitting under the field you're typing in. The top fade stays, because that edge still runs under the header.
  - Wired with a capture-phase `scroll` listener (scroll doesn't bubble) plus a ResizeObserver and MutationObserver per scroller, coalesced into one frame. No call needed at each render site, and a screen that measures zero while hidden gets its fades the moment it opens and gains height.
- **Sizes are the one private thing** — and hiding them on someone else's profile is a real cost, since their size is exactly what you'd want when offering a swap. Kept it anyway; it's the rule doing actual work.
- **About me is capped at 500 characters, with a live counter.** For scale, the longest bio in the data is 326 and the rest are around 100.
- **The box is sized to the cap, not the other way round.** 296px is 13 lines at this measure, which is exactly what a full 500 characters takes — so hitting the limit and running out of visible box happen at the same moment. A counter that says you have room while the field has started scrolling is telling you two different things.
- The counter reads "326 / 400" — what you wrote, then the ceiling. Counting *up* keeps the useful number first; the ceiling only matters as you approach it, and that's when the whole line turns the destructive colour.
- `maxlength` stops typing and pasting but not a value set any other way, so the cap is applied once more on save.
- **Per-section editing, not a global edit mode.** The sections hold different kinds of thing — prose, a curated tag set, constrained values — so they get three different editors behind one sheet. A single "Edit Profile" button would have to open all three. (The old one was a lie: it opened the sizes sheet.)
- **Wardrobe tags are derived from your own listings**, most-used first, capped at 12. Nobody writes them, so there's no edit button. A closet describes itself; a hand-kept copy drifts from it.
- **The top-right slot splits by whose profile it is.** Yours gets a gear — settings are things you *change* (post radius, username, pronouns, password), which is a screen. Theirs keeps the `...` and opens mute / block / report — actions you take *about* someone, which is a menu. Same slot, two different kinds of thing, so they shouldn't share a glyph.
- **Block and report are tinted with the destructive token**, mute isn't. Muting is a preference; the other two have consequences.
- **Every tag on a profile is a way in** — tap it and home filters to it. One rule for both sections and both profiles. On someone else's, "what they have" and "what they want" are the two things worth browsing.
- **Collapsing header only collapses when the page can sustain it.** Collapsing hands 131px back to the body, which *shortens* the scroll range by that much. On a short tab that left less range than the trigger needed: scroll past 56 → collapse → position clamps under 24 → expand → loop. You got bounced back to the top instead of reaching the bottom.

## Cards, and the marquee

- **Grid titles are always one line.** When one doesn't fit it holds still, tracks left to reveal the rest, holds, and comes back.
- **Constant speed, not constant duration.** Duration is computed per card from how far the title actually overruns (~28px/sec), so a title that overflows by 102px and one that overflows by 127px travel at the same rate — they just take different amounts of time (10.7s vs 13.3s). Sharing one duration would have made the long one visibly faster, and the eye reads *speed*, not elapsed time. This is the detail worth writing up.
- **Only titles that actually overrun get it.** A title that fits and still slid around would be motion for its own sake.
- **The fades are tied to travel, not painted on.** The leading fade is off while the title rests at its start; the trailing fade switches off once the tail is fully in view. A static both-sides mask would dim text that has nothing hidden past it. Both fades run on the same clock as the travel, so they can't drift.
- **Two CSS traps this exposed**, both of which silently reported "it fits":
  - An `inline-block` shrink-to-fits to the space available, so the text measured the *card's* width, not its own. Needs `width:max-content`.
  - A `1fr` grid track won't go below its content's min-content width — and once the title was `nowrap`, its min-content became the entire string, so the column stretched to 275px and blew out the two-column layout. Needs `minmax(0,1fr)`.

## Post pages

- Order is username → title → photos → description → size → tags, on both the detail page and the feed.
- **Photo rail bleeds past the content column on both sides.** At rest the first photo squares up with the title; scrolled, photos run to the screen edge instead of vanishing into a gutter 16px in. Snap points ignore padding unless you set `scroll-padding` — without it the rail scrolls *itself* 16px on load and the first photo sits off the text column.
- **Your own posts lose the heart and Message**, everywhere they appear — detail, feed, grid. They get an edit pencil instead, in the slot where the chevron into someone's profile would be.
- Tags dropped from the feed card; the size caption carries the row alone.
- **The slot beside the poster's name splits the same way the profile's does.** Yours is a pencil into the editor; theirs is a `...` opening hide post / block user / report post. One slot, two different kinds of thing — a way to change the post vs. a way to act on it — so they never share a glyph. Same rule, same menu component, same danger tint on the bottom two rows.
- **Hide actually hides.** Of the three it's the only one whose result you'd expect to see, and a menu item that names a visible outcome and produces none reads as broken. It drops out of the feed, the grid, the map and search at once, because all four read from `searchable()` — the same single-list invariant that keeps search from offering dead ends. Hiding the post you're currently reading pops you back out of it.
- **Undo lives on the toast**, and hiding is the reason the toast learned to carry an action at all: a card vanishing with no way back is a trap in something you demo. Action toasts also live 5s instead of 2.2s — long enough to read a confirmation isn't long enough to notice, reach and decide. They're the only toasts that take pointer events; the plain ones stay inert so they can't eat a tap meant for the page.
- A hidden post still appears on its owner's profile and in your Faves. Hiding is a feed action, not a delete.
- **The menu anchors off a client rect, not layout offsets** — the opposite of the rule everywhere else. A feed card's `...` lives in a scrolling list, where `offsetTop` describes where the button *would* be unscrolled. The rect knows; it's just in scaled pixels, so the frame's own scale gets divided back out. It flips above the anchor rather than running off the bottom.

## Post flow

- **Editing a post is now the composer, arriving full** — title, description, type, size, cut, tags and photo tiles all prefilled, landing on the details step since the words are what you usually came to change. It used to open empty, which read as posting and set a trap: back on the empty photo step opened the picker with nothing selected and Add disabled, so the only exit threw your "work" away. And publishing from there *duplicated* the post and yanked you to the feed.
- Save (the button says Save, not Publish) writes back onto the same record — same id, owner, place — re-renders everywhere the post appears, and leaves you exactly where you were, trail intact. Back off the photo step exits to the post when editing; only a fresh post treats the picker as the step before it.

- **Posting starts in the OS, not in Bloom.** The plus opens an iOS action sheet (Photo Gallery / Camera / Cancel), gallery opens a mimic of the system picker, and only then does our composer open. That's where a real user's first thirty seconds actually go — starting the prototype at an "Upload images" grid would test a step nobody performs.
- **The system screens deliberately use none of our tokens.** System font, Apple's blue, Apple's metrics. Anything wearing our type would read as *our* screen and set the wrong expectation about what it can do.
- **Photos carry through by identity, not by count.** The flow used to track "how many", so the composer showed a fresh set of placeholders — you'd pick a blush jacket and get a sand tee. It now passes the actual tiles, in pick order, through the upload grid, the rail, and the preview.
- **Selection is capped at six**, because that's what the composer has slots for. At the limit the unpicked cells go dim rather than silently swallowing the tap — that's what iOS does with a selection limit, and a tap that does nothing reads as broken.
- **Any `i % 5` scheme stripes a 3-column grid.** Tint and glyph both cycling mod 5 repeat every 5 cells, which lands as diagonal bands. Both are drawn from an irrational-stride sequence instead — well spread, no visible period, same every run.
- **The upload step shows only what you have, plus one way to add more.** The fixed six-slot grid rendered five empty cards — five things you appeared to have failed to do. Now it's your photos and a single dashed `+` tile, which disappears at six. The cap lives in a caption that counts ("3 of 6 photos added"), so the limit is stated before you hit it rather than enforced after.
- **Type, then size.** A size means nothing until you know what it's sizing, so the composer asks for the garment type first and reveals the matching size picker only once you've answered. The picker is built from the same `CATEGORIES` / `SIZE_TIERS` that search and My Sizes read, so a posted size is always one a filter can match — the free-text field it replaced let you write something no search would ever find. Bottoms get both tiers (letter and waist), ruled apart because they're alternatives; accessories get "Optional" and the add-a-size hatch, since a ring or hat size is real even though most accessories have none.
- **Posting now sets `cat` and `fit`, not just a display string.** Without them a new post landed in the feed but no category chip or size filter could reach it — it existed and was unfindable. Verified: a size-30 bottoms post shows up under a Bottoms + "M" filter, via the letter-to-waist mapping.
- **Cut (Mens / Womens / Unisex) is optional and only appears where it changes what a size means** — tops, bottoms, outerwear, shoes. A dress size is a dress size; an M in a men's tee is not an M in a women's. Hats and accessories don't get it because they don't carry a size system at all.
- It sits under the whole size picker rather than between the tiers and their own add button — it qualifies the size, so it reads as the last thing you say about it, and splitting the tiers from their `+ add size` would have been worse.
- Being optional, it's the one chip group where tapping the selected one turns it back off. Type is required, so it doesn't behave that way.
- It composes into the display string the seeded listings already use — "Womens Size 8" — while `cut` and `fit` stay separate on the record.
- Changing type keeps the size when the new type still offers it (tops → dresses keeps M) and drops it when it doesn't (bottoms 32 → shoes).
- **"+ add size" for the sizes no tier can express**, built like the "I'm looking for" editor: a field, live suggestions, your additions landing in the row above. It differs from that editor in one deliberate way — **it lets you commit what you typed**. A tag you invented is a saved filter that finds nothing; a size you invented is a fact about the garment in your hands, and refusing to let you state it is worse than the filter miss it costs. The field offers only what you typed. It briefly suggested non-standard sizes borrowed from other listings, which put a chip under the field that looked like a size appearing by itself — and it only turned up for Tops, the one category with such a fit in the data, so it read as a glitch. Captioning it helped and still wasn't worth it: the escape hatch is for the size in your hands, not for browsing.
- The data already had the case: a 34B bralette, `cat:'tops'`, that the letter tier could never have posted.
- **One custom size per type, and it's removable.** A garment has one size, so once you've added one the plus goes away — the way to change it is to X it out, not to stack another beside it. The chip carries the label and the X as separate buttons so neither steals the other's tap, and it stays pickable: choose a standard size and your custom one dims rather than vanishing.
- **The bar only ever grows sideways.** The field opens on the chip's own top edge, at the chip's exact height, and closes back into whatever now occupies that slot — no vertical travel, no height change, at any tag count. Three things were moving it: the entry block's 8px top padding; the plus trailing the tag chips inside a wrapping row, so the field replacing it opened wherever that row happened to end; and a flourish where a commit collapsed the field into the chip it had just made, a row above. And a fourth, which only showed on Accessories: that's the one type whose size section opens with a caption instead of tier rows, and the caption's 6px bottom margin collapsed into the field's 8px top margin when closed but *not* when open — the empty chip row left standing in between is `display:flex`, so it establishes a formatting context and margins can't collapse through it. The caption lost its margin; the row below owns the gap. The plus now gets its own line, the entry inherits the spacing class of the row it stands in for, and the field always closes into its own slot. Measured 0px on both axes across all six types and 0–5 tags, on open, on cancel and on commit.
- **What you're typing previews in the row it's going to land in**, dashed until it's real, rather than as a "+ 'fff'" proposal under the field. You watch the set you're building instead of a suggestion detached from it, and committing changes a chip's state instead of moving it somewhere else. Tapping the pending chip commits it, same as Enter.
  - This moves the layout shift rather than adding one. Before, the first keystroke swapped the hint for a chip row *and* committing pushed everything down as the tag arrived above. Now the hint below is constant and all the movement is on the keystroke that creates the row: 41px once, then nothing — **commit is 0px**, which is the moment you're actually watching.
  - The 8px that used to arrive at commit was `.flow-more:not(:empty)`, the gap that belongs to a row with something in it. While you're typing, the pending chip *is* that something, so the gap comes with it.
  - A placeholder `<span>` for the preview to render into was the obvious build and it silently cost 8px: an empty span still makes the row non-`:empty`, so the field dropped the moment it opened. The chip is inserted and removed directly instead.
- **Choose Location draws the post's radius on a map.** A circle over streets says how far a post carries in one look; "within 1 mile" doesn't. It reuses the feed's baked basemap and the same Web Mercator projection at a smaller scale, so there's one map in the app rather than two that can disagree. The tint moved into a `--map-skin` token when the second map needed it.
  - **A zip has to resolve to a neighborhood before it's stored.** Typing 11385 shows "Ridgewood, NY", because a raw "11385" on a listing is a string no pin, no filter and no other post can ever match. Enough of a geocoder for the places the app knows, and unrecognised text falls back rather than being saved as-is.
  - **The radius is set by what the basemap can honestly draw.** It sat at 1 mile while the sheet was 8.3km across, because a 2-mile circle around Ridgewood ran off the eastern edge of the image and drawing it anyway would have been a picture lying about its own coverage. Re-baking the map wider (below) raised it to 2.
  - **The basemap was re-baked wider, and changed source doing it.** Reading `bake_map.py` turned up that the original crop kept barely half of what had been stitched — the discarded half was most of the area now wanted. But CARTO's tiles now come back stamped "API KEY REQUIRED" diagonally across every one; the shipped image predated that. Swapped to **Esri's Dark Gray Canvas base layer**: no key, no account, and no baked-in street names — CARTO's had "Long Island Expressway" and "BROOKLYN" burnt into the tiles, competing with our own type at every zoom. Esri serves 256px tiles, so it bakes one zoom deeper to land on the same ground-per-pixel.
  - **The tint was re-fitted by measurement, not by eye.** Esri's ground sits at 71/255 with streets at 96; CARTO's sat at 4 and 41. Solving `brightness × contrast` to map those two points exactly onto the old numbers means every step after it produces the same palette as before — the map changed source without changing appearance. Tile ranges are now derived from the bbox rather than hardcoded, so moving the frame is a two-line edit.
  - The wider sheet also made the map *smaller*: 264 KB of data URI against the old 143 KB for 2.5× the ground, because Esri's label-free tiles compress far better. `index.html` went 384 KB → 490 KB.
  - **Water is lifted at bake time, because the CSS reskin can't reach it.** That filter is a function of luminance, and Esri draws water *darker* than land (35 vs 71) where CARTO drew it brighter — so the chain that made rivers pale pink turned them to pitch. A lookup table moves water into the band above the streets and the old relationship comes back. Safe to do by threshold: the histogram is two spikes with a hole between them — water at 35 ± 2, land from 65, under 1% in between. That 1% is antialiased shoreline, ramped rather than stepped so the coast doesn't halo.

- **The pins were drawing a lattice, not a scatter.** They came out in clean diagonal chains that read as a rendering artifact. The cause was the offset function: an LCG (`(n*9301+49297) % 233280`) seeded with ids 1–27. At that scale an LCG doesn't randomise, it draws a grid — consecutive seeds give proportional outputs — and the two axes were seeded `id` and `id+7`, near neighbours, which is what tilted the rows into diagonals. A bit-mixing hash has no such structure at small inputs.
- **Then the scatter was replaced outright with a golden-angle spiral.** Fixing the hash removed the chains but not the clumping — random points clump by nature, and nine of nineteen pins still overlapped, where a clump reads as one pin rather than as several. A neighborhood's listings now sit on a phyllotaxis spiral around its centre: evenly spaced at any count, no per-hood tuning, furthest about 1.4km out so it's still inside the neighborhood it claims. The hash survives as a small nudge off each slot, so it reads as scatter rather than as a flower. 0 overlaps, nearest-neighbour median 70px against a 34px pin.
  - The lng offset is divided by `cos(lat)`, or the spiral comes out squashed east-west: longitude degrees are shorter than latitude degrees at this latitude, and the projection is conformal.
- **Three neighborhoods were added once the map could show them** — Dumbo, Lower East Side, Park Slope — all beyond the old sheet's edges. Rather than inventing people to live in them, existing users moved: one person still lives in one place, which is what their profile claims and what their whole closet being in one spot depends on. `ines_k` → Lower East Side, `tasha_p` → Park Slope, and Sally → Dumbo, which also settled an old inconsistency where her profile said Bushwick while all nine of her listings said "Brooklyn, NY".
  - The ring and the dot sit *inside* the map sheet, positioned geographically like the feed's pins. The sheet is clamped so its edge never shows, and because the circle is pinned to the ground rather than to the panel, clamping slides the map under a stationary circle instead of dragging the circle off the place it describes. Verified across all six neighborhoods: no exposed edge, no clipped ring.
  - Panel height is derived from the radius, not fixed, so changing the mileage re-frames the map instead of cropping it.
  - **The map's pan clamp was measuring in two different units** — `getBoundingClientRect()` on a phone that's CSS-transform scaled, compared against `SHEET.w/h` in layout px. It let a full drag run about 44px past the sheet's own edge and expose the ground behind it. Pre-existing, found while re-checking the clamp against the new sheet size. `clientWidth/clientHeight` are unscaled, and are what the comparison wanted.
- **The preview step shows the post as one object, in a white card.** It was loose parts lying on the composer's cream, which is how the *form* is built — so the last step looked like more form rather than like the thing the form makes.
  - `overflow:hidden` on that card is load-bearing, not corner-rounding. The photo rail deliberately bleeds past the text column so the next photo peeks out; in the feed it peeks past the screen edge, and here the card is what it peeks past. Without the clip the rail runs out over the cream and the card stops reading as a card. Verified in both scroll directions — photos clip at the card's edge, not the screen's.
  - The feed's `border-bottom` on `.post` comes off inside the preview: that rule divides one post from the next, and there's exactly one here.
  - **Tags show in the preview, and only there.** They were the one thing you entered that the preview didn't hand back, so you could get them wrong and find out on the live post. The feed keeps them off — a third row of chrome on every card in a long scroll, and the post you tap into shows them anyway. Same `.listing-tags` row as the post page, so what you check is what you get. No tags means no row rather than an empty one or a "No tags" chip: the composer's caption already said they were optional, and a placeholder chip in a preview of *your* post would be showing you something that isn't in it.
- **The x asks before it throws work away** — the only control in the composer that destroys something. It reuses the same confirm dialog as un-faving, with a danger-weighted Discard.
  - It asks only when there's something to lose, and "something" means two different things. Posting, a fresh composer starts empty, so anything in it counts — photos picked, words typed, a type chosen. Editing, the composer arrives *full*, so that test would make the x ask every single time; what counts there is whether anything differs from the post you opened. Add a tag and remove it again and it closes without a word.
  - Back off step 1 while editing leaves by the same door — the picker isn't behind you when the composer arrived full — so it asks the same question rather than being a silent way around the guard.
- **The tag field stays open across commits; the size field doesn't.** Tags come in runs, so it hands itself back empty and focused and the only thing that closes it is you — the X or Escape. A size is one value, so committing it is the end of the interaction. Same control, and the difference between them is exactly the difference between the two lists.
- An Enter on an empty field does nothing rather than closing — a stray return shouldn't dismiss anything.
- **Ten tags max, stated by a caption that counts** — same shape as the photo step's ("4 of 10 tags added"), so the limit arrives before you reach it and, at the cap, explains where the plus went. The tenth tag closes the field, since there's nothing left to type; it closes flat rather than morphing, because at ten there's no plus to collapse into and aiming at the new chip a row above would take the bar off its line. Removing one brings the plus back.
- **The chip-becomes-a-field control is used twice**, for the one size and for any number of tags, as one piece of code rather than two that drift. Tags replaced a browser `prompt()` — the last piece of borrowed OS chrome inside our own screens — and each tag chip gained the same X.
- **The chip morphs into the field** — the variant that lost the "+ add tag" A/B test, revisited here because the situation is different: this field appears mid-form rather than at the top of a screen, so something has to explain where it came from. Plain FLIP: measure the chip, re-render, animate the field from the chip's box (240ms) with the contents fading in at 45%. The field wears the chip's own border, fill, 8px corners and exact height — no search icon, nothing pill-shaped — so the morph is a change of width and nothing else. Getting the heights to match to the pixel took zeroing the input's UA padding and pinning its line-height; 2px off would have popped at the end of the move. Closing is the same move measured in reverse — the chip animates out of where the field was. Travel is exactly symmetric: (56, −41) out, (−56, 41) back.
- **Two measurement traps in that morph**, both the usual kind:
  - The chip and the field sit under different positioned ancestors, so raw `offsetLeft/Top` aren't in the same coordinate space. Had to accumulate the whole offset chain.
  - Measuring an element with a morph still in flight returns its *animated* width — tapping twice quickly measured a half-open field instead of a chip. `boxOf` now cancels running animations before it measures.
- Typing a size the tiers already have ("M" for tops) selects the existing chip rather than adding a duplicate; a repeated tag is ignored the same way.
- Tags typed here join the search vocabulary, since `styleTags()` walks the listings. That's fine where a saved *search* tag wouldn't be: a tag on your own post describes something that exists, so it can't become a filter that finds nothing.
- **Naming collision worth remembering:** the composer's size rows reused `.size-tier` from the My Sizes screen, which is padded 16px to sit inside a card. The chips ended up inset from the Type row above them for no visible reason. Renamed to `.flow-tier`.
- **The composer's photo rail bleeds like a post's does.** Same technique — cancel the body padding with a negative margin, re-add it inside — so the first tile still squares up with the headings while the ones leaving run off the screen edge. Tiles went 118px → 140px because at 118 the third one landed flush with the frame and read as a tangent rather than as a cut-off photo.
- **The description step's photos carry no controls at all.** There the rail is context for what you're writing, not a task, so the pencils came off the thumbnails entirely — tapping one opens it full size in the same lightbox a listing uses, and the pencil lives down there, bottom-right, mirroring the close button's corner so the counter stays centred between them. The upload step keeps its always-on pencils, because there the photos *are* the task. It also fixed a quieter problem: those thumbnail pencils were inert on this step, since only the upload grid's tiles were ever wired up.
- **The pencil opens a crop-and-rotate editor** — mocked, but reachable and dismissible so the flow demos end to end. Crop and rotate is what a pencil on a photo is expected to mean, so the alternative (bouncing back out to the picker) would have been the pencil lying about itself; adding and removing photos is the button up on the description step.
- **Tapping a photo means the same thing on both steps: open it full size.** The upload step used to reopen the picker instead, so the same gesture meant two things depending on which step you were on. Now the lightbox is the per-photo surface and the controls that act on one photo live there — remove bottom-left, crop bottom-right, mirroring each other across the counter. The tile's own pencil stays as a shortcut straight to the editor.
- **Moving the photo tap forced the remove control.** Reopening the picker *was* the removal route — deselect and Add — and at six photos there's no `+` tile either, so the change would have left no way to drop a photo at all. Removing from the lightbox is more direct anyway: it acts on the photo you're looking at, keeps you in place with the index clamped, and closes only when the last one goes. No confirm, unlike a fave: a dropped photo is one tap from being re-picked, and a dialog per photo would sit in the middle of the flow.
- Rotate and flip actually move the preview, because they're two CSS values and they're what makes the screen read as live rather than as a screenshot. Aspect chips retarget the crop frame. None of it is written back to the post, and reopening resets.
- **Name collision worth remembering:** `openEditor`/`closeEditor` were already the profile's section editors. The `const` threw loudly, but the `function` would have silently shadowed the profile one — a redeclared function declaration just wins. Renamed to `openPhotoEditor`.
- **The lightbox now takes tiles, not a rail to read them off.** A listing's shots are one garment at stepped tones; the composer's are whatever you picked, each its own tint and glyph. Only the caller knows which, so the caller builds the list. That's also what gates the pencil — there's nothing to change about someone else's photo.
- **"Edit photos" became "Change".** "Edit" promises retouching — crop, filter, rotate — and the step it opens is where you add and remove. Paired with the heading it reads "Photos … Change", which covers both without listing them, and the icon went from a pencil to a photo for the same reason.
- The `+` tile and the Gallery/Camera buttons it replaced do the same thing — hand back to the OS. Tapping a photo you already added reopens the picker with the current selection made, which is where you'd swap one out. The app never picks photos itself, at any depth.
- **The flow wears the editing-screen head**, not chrome of its own: back arrow, maroon italic title, close. It *is* editing a post, and its own dark bar made it read as a separate mode. Section headings went maroon to match too.
- **Back off the first step returns to the picker, not out of posting.** The picker is genuinely the step before it. The composer stays open underneath, which gives the picker's Cancel a meaning — "keep what I had" — and reopening it carries the current selection back in, numbered, because going back isn't starting over.

## Bugs worth remembering

These cost the most time and are the most transferable.

- **Measuring an element on a hidden screen returns zero, and zero reads as a valid answer.** Bit three times: the profile header's clamp measured 0 and clamped itself shut; the collapse metric cached a wrong height; the marquees silently disarmed because "0 wide" looked like "it fits". Every measurement in a render function needs a zero guard, because renders often run *before* the screen is shown.
- **Client rects and layout pixels don't mix inside a scaled frame.** `getBoundingClientRect` is scaled by the phone frame's transform; `offsetHeight`/`offsetWidth` aren't. Anything written back into CSS has to come from the latter. Bit the body-shift measurement and the view menu's anchor — the menu only landed correctly when the frame happened to render at 1:1.
- **Pointer capture retargets the trailing click to the capturing element.** The map captured on `pointerdown` so drags could track outside the element — which meant every tap on a pin arrived as a tap on the *map*, hit the dismiss handler, and closed the callout instead of opening it. Every pin, every time. Fix: capture only once the gesture passes the drag threshold.
- **A cancelled Web Animation *rejects* its `finished` promise.** So an interrupted transition never ran its cleanup, and the departing screen kept its "still on stage" class — sitting there frozen until some later transition happened to involve it again. Each slide now registers its cleanup and the next one runs it before cancelling.
- **Duplicate ids across two arrays.** Added listings numbered 15–18 without noticing another array already used 15–18. `find()` returns the first match, so four of one person's items opened someone else's listing, and favouriting one toggled the wrong heart. All ids unique now.
- **A permanently-present element toggling `hidden` breaks existence checks.** Verified map callouts with `!!querySelector('.callout')` for a long stretch — always true, tested nothing. Check `.hidden`, not presence.

## Events

- **The one screen that isn't about a garment, so the one that looks like the brand** rather than like a form: the wordmark and flower up top, three colourways in the rail, and a slow gradient instead of a flat ground.
- **The gradient is five oversized radial blobs, each drifting on its own clock.** Only `transform` animates, so it lives entirely on the compositor — no repaints, no `requestAnimationFrame`, no canvas, about five divs. Three details decide whether it reads as a gradient or as circles:
  - The blobs are 170% of the screen, so an edge never enters frame.
  - The softness is the gradient's own transparent falloff, **not** `filter:blur()` — a blurred full-screen layer is the one thing here that would cost frames on a phone.
  - The durations (11/13/17/19/23s) are all prime, so the composition never visibly returns to where it started — the five loops only realign after about ten hours.
  - **Speed alone didn't make it read as motion; distance did.** Halving the durations made it hurried without making it more visible. What registers is how far the composition travels: the blobs now cross 350–415px on a 390px screen, roughly double, and a glance six seconds apart looks like a different picture.
  - Sized in `%`, not `vw`: `vw` is the browser window, and this is a 390px phone drawn inside it — in `vw` the blobs grew and shrank with the desktop window instead of holding still relative to the device. The same units trap as the map's pan clamp, one layer up.
- **The ground is plum, and that decides the mark.** The first build took the reference images literally — sandy, peach, pastel — and the brand cyan measured about 1:1 on it and vanished outright. Berry read fine there, but the Figma pairs a cyan mark with a plum page, and that pairing is the one the mark was drawn for. Matching the Figma's ground put the cyan back at 5.4:1 without touching either colour.
- **Grain is one tiled SVG turbulence, not an image asset.** The browser renders the 200px tile once and it's a repeating background after that — nothing per frame, nothing to download, and the density is a number in the CSS rather than a re-export. Desaturated, because coloured noise reads as a broken JPEG. It deliberately does *not* move with the blobs: grain that drifts stops looking like grain and starts looking like a dirty lens.
  - `overlay` at .38 read as clean — overlay with mid-grey noise tends toward neutral, so on deep colours it needs pushing much further than it would on a light ground. .8 is where the texture actually arrives.
  - It sits inside `.ev-bg`, so it grains the field and leaves the cards clean. The reference image grains everything; here the cards carry the text.
- **Saturating the palette broke the header, quietly.** Pushing the teal brighter took cyan-on-teal from 3.28:1 to 2.65 — under the 3:1 bar for display type — and it's the one blob that drifts under the wordmark. Its lightness is now a cap rather than a choice: saturation is free on that blob, lightness isn't. Back to 3.33:1 at the worst point of the cycle.
- **The mark survives the whole drift cycle.** Cyan on the deepest blob is 9.3:1 and on the palest 2.78:1 — but that pale rose never reaches the header band. Scrubbing each blob's animation and testing overlap, the worst that actually passes under the wordmark is the teal at 3.28:1, over the 3:1 threshold for display type. Worth checking rather than assuming: on a moving background, contrast is a range, not a number.
- **Blobs are a family around the ground, not a spectrum across it.** Their luminances sit between 0.044 and 0.201 either side of the page's 0.095, so it reads as one field breathing rather than five coloured circles crossing. The single cool note — the brand cyan at 12% — is what the reference images do when they turn blue in one corner without ever stopping being one colour.
- Two things had assumed a light ground and had to follow: the scrim under the nav pill, which fades content into white everywhere else and read as a grey haze over plum; and the status bar, which had this screen down as cream.
- The plum card lifted from `--berry` to a lighter mauve. On a berry-family page a berry card disappears — in the Figma that first card is a lift *out of* the background, not the background repeated.
- `--bloom` is its own token rather than folded into `--sky` (`#8DD9F0`). They're close enough to look like a mistake side by side and far enough apart to be one, so the logo's colour is named as the logo's.
- The export's `clipPath` was dropped on inlining: its rect was exactly the viewBox, which inline SVG clips to anyway, and keeping it would have put a generated id (`clip0_61_1413`) into the document's global id space. Fills became `currentColor` so a mark can take berry here and cyan on ink elsewhere.
- **Upcoming Events leads, My Tickets follows.** A ticket you already hold is a reference you come back to; the reason to open this tab is what's coming. The ticket card also went opaque white — on a moving gradient, a tinted panel let the blobs drift through the one thing on the screen that's actually yours.
- Three card colourways rather than two, so the rail reads as a set rather than an alternation, and the middle card isn't always the dark one.
